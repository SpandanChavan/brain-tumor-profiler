## Intended use
- **Primary:** research, teaching, and demonstrating AI-assisted tumor segmentation.
- **Users:** students, researchers, clinicians *exploring* the technology.
- **Out of scope:** diagnosis, treatment planning, surgical or radiotherapy targeting, any patient-facing use. This is not a medical device (FDA SaMD / CDSCO MDR 2017).

## Ethical considerations
- **Data:** BraTS is de-identified and skull-stripped. Its population comes mostly from North American and European centres. Performance on Indian or other populations, children, and non-glioma lesions is **unknown**.
- **Automation bias:** the UI shows uncertainty, contour mode, and "clinical review required" wording to stop people over-trusting it.
- **Privacy:** see `docs/risk_assessment.md`. The design is HIPAA-aligned, not certified compliant.

## Caveats
- 2D slice model: less 3D context than full-volume networks.
- Sensitive to preprocessing: non-skull-stripped or non-registered scans degrade results. The app warns about this.
- Small tumors and boundaries cause the most errors (see the worst-case gallery in the report).
